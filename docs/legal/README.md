# Terms & Privacy — what's left before these go live

Two drafts live here: `terms-of-service.md` and `privacy-policy.md`. They
are **not on the site yet** — publishing a legal page with blanks in it is
worse than having none. Fill in the blanks below, then ask Claude to turn
both into `/terms` and `/privacy` pages with links in the site footer and a
"By creating an account you agree to the Terms" line on the sign-up form.

This is not legal advice. Once Plus is actually being sold, paying a lawyer
for an hour's review is money well spent — especially on refunds and the
liability cap.

## Decisions only the owner can make

1. **Who is "we"?** Your own name, or an LLC? (An LLC keeps a lawsuit
   against the site away from your personal savings. Worth doing before you
   take payments.)
2. **Contact email** for legal/privacy requests (e.g. privacy@pricingthemagic.com).
3. **Governing law**: which state and county.
4. **Minimum age** for an account: 16 or 18? (13 is the legal floor in the
   US; 18 avoids questions about minors making purchases.)
5. **Plus price and renewal**: $9/90 days and $19/year are what CLAUDE.md
   records. Does it auto-renew, or just end? (CLAUDE.md's reasoning favors
   "just ends" — people plan a trip every 1-3 years.)
6. **Refund policy**: suggested "full refund within 14 days, no questions".
7. **How long to keep anonymous searched routes** (suggested: 2 years).

## Things the app needs before these promises are true

These are claims the drafts make that the code doesn't back yet:

- **An unsubscribe link in deal emails.** US law (CAN-SPAM) requires one
  in marketing email. Deal emails currently have none.
- **A way to delete your account.** Right now it's "email us" and Claude
  deletes it by hand — acceptable at launch, but the policy says so.
- **A "not affiliated with Disney" line on the site itself**, not just in
  the terms. There is none today. Recommended in the footer of every page
  and on the PDF.
- **Affiliate disclosure near the links**, once any link carries an
  affiliate tag (the FTC wants it near the link, not only in the terms).
